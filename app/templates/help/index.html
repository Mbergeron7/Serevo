Getting Started

▸

First-Time Setup

When you first access {{ brand_name }}, you'll see the setup page if no admin account exists yet. Create your admin account by entering your email and a strong password. This becomes the primary administrator account.

After creating the admin account, you should immediately:

  1. Go to Settings → General and set your organization's brand name, accent color, and tagline.
  2. Go to Settings → Activities and configure the activities your workforce performs (calls, emails, breaks, lunch, etc.).
  3. Go to Settings → Contracts and set up at least one contract template (e.g., Full-Time 40hr).
  4. Go to Settings → Planning Units and create your lines of business.
  5. Go to Settings → Day Models and create your shift templates.
  6. Go to Settings → Users to add supervisor and agent accounts.
  7. Go to People and add your employees, assigning them contracts, skills, and planning units.

Logging In

Navigate to your {{ brand_name }} URL and enter your email and password. If your organization has Google or Microsoft sign-in enabled, you can use the social login buttons instead.

Sessions last 8 hours. After that, you'll need to sign in again.

Navigation

The top navigation bar shows all available sections based on your role. Admins see all sections. Supervisors see most sections. Agents see only their personal portal (My Schedule, My Time Off, Self-Service).

Use the search bar (or press Ctrl+K / ⌘K) to quickly find employees, pages, or actions.

Notifications

Click the bell icon in the top right to see your notifications. These include PTO requests, schedule changes, shift swap requests, shift bidding results, and system alerts. Notifications are automatically marked as read when you open them.

Install as an App

{{ brand_name }} can be installed on your phone or computer for quick access — no app store needed.

Android (Chrome): Tap the three-dot menu and select Add to Home Screen. You'll get an app icon that opens {{ brand_name }} in its own window.

iPhone / iPad (Safari): Tap the share button (square with arrow), then tap Add to Home Screen.

Desktop (Chrome): Look for the install icon in the right side of the address bar and click it to pin {{ brand_name }} as a desktop app.

User Roles & Permissions

▸

Admin

Full access to every feature in {{ brand_name }}. Admins can:

  • Create, edit, and deactivate user accounts and assign roles
  • Configure all system settings (activities, contracts, planning units, day models, skills, schedule rules, API connections, Google Sheets, OAuth)
  • Add, edit, and remove employees from the roster
  • Build, generate, and publish schedules
  • Approve or deny PTO requests, shift swaps, and other approval workflows
  • Post open shifts for bidding, VTO opportunities, and OT opportunities
  • Create and manage forecasts, capacity plans, and long-range plans
  • View and manage real-time adherence, attendance, quality, payroll, reports, and training
  • Access the audit log to review all system actions
  • Post announcements and manage documents

Supervisor

View and limited-edit access to most management sections. Supervisors can:

  • View the Dashboard, People, Scheduling, Forecasting, Capacity, Real-Time, Attendance, Quality, Reports, Payroll, Training, Shift Notes, Documents, and Announcements pages
  • Approve or deny PTO requests and shift swap requests
  • Approve or deny shift bidding selections (if delegated by admin)
  • Create and edit schedules for their team
  • Post shift notes and announcements
  • Access their own agent portal (My Schedule, My Time Off, clock in/out)

Supervisors cannot change system settings, manage user accounts, or access the audit log.

Agent

Access is limited to the personal agent portal. Agents can:

  • View their own schedule in My Schedule
  • Request time off in My Time Off and view request status
  • Clock in and out from the Self-Service portal
  • Browse and bid on open shifts posted by admins/supervisors
  • Request shift swaps with other agents or accept open swap requests
  • Sign up for VTO (voluntary time off) or OT (overtime) opportunities
  • View announcements and personal training assignments
  • Submit WFM support tickets

Agents cannot access management pages, view other employees' data, or change settings.

Managing Users

Go to Settings → Users to manage user accounts:

  1. Click + Add User.
  2. Enter the user's email address and choose a role (Admin, Supervisor, or Agent).
  3. Optionally link the user account to an existing employee record. This is required for agents so they can see their own schedule, submit PTO, clock in/out, and participate in shift swaps and bidding.
  4. Click Save. The user will receive login credentials.

To edit a user, click their row in the user list. You can change their role, update their email, link/unlink an employee record, or deactivate the account. Deactivated accounts cannot log in but their data is preserved.

Dashboard

▸

The Dashboard is the landing page for admins and supervisors. It provides a real-time snapshot of your workforce.

KPI Cards

The top row displays key metrics that update in real time:

  • Total Active Employees — count of employees with an active status
  • Scheduled Today — how many employees have shifts assigned for today
  • Pending PTO Requests — number of time-off requests awaiting approval

Charts

Below the KPI cards you'll find interactive charts:

  • Headcount Trend — active employee count over the selected time range
  • Attendance Rate — daily attendance percentage with absence breakdowns
  • Hours Worked vs. Scheduled — comparison of actual vs. planned hours

Hover over any data point to see exact values. Use the date range selector to change the time window.

Recent Activity

The activity feed shows the latest changes across the system: new hires, schedule publications, approved/denied PTO, shift swap completions, configuration changes, and more. Each entry shows the action, the user who performed it, and a timestamp.

People (Roster)

▸

Viewing the Roster

The People page shows all employees in a sortable, filterable table. Use the filter controls at the top to narrow the list:

  • Search by Name — type any part of the employee's first or last name
  • LOB / Planning Unit — filter to a specific line of business
  • Status — filter by Active, Inactive, On Leave, or Terminated
  • Team Lead — show only employees assigned to a specific team lead
  • Contract — filter by contract type (e.g., Full-Time, Part-Time)

Click any column header to sort ascending/descending. Choose how many employees to show per page (25, 50, 100, or All) using the page-size selector, and use Previous/Next buttons to navigate.

Adding an Employee

  1. Click + Add Employee at the top of the roster.
  2. Fill in required fields: First Name, Last Name, Employee ID, and Status.
  3. Set optional fields: hire date, email, phone, LOB/planning unit, team lead, contract type, weekly hours target.
  4. Click Save. The employee is now in the roster and can be scheduled.

Editing an Employee

Click on any employee row to open their profile. Click the Edit button to modify their details. Changes are saved when you click Save. All edits are tracked in the audit log.

Employee Profile Details

Each employee's profile page contains several tabs/sections:

  • Overview — name, ID, contact info, status, hire date, LOB, team lead
  • Schedule History — past and upcoming shift assignments
  • PTO History — all time-off requests and their statuses
  • Documents — uploaded files (contracts, certifications, ID copies)
  • Training — assigned training modules and completion status
  • Quality — quality evaluation scores over time
  • Accommodations — any scheduling accommodations on file

Contract Assignment

Each employee should be assigned a contract. The contract controls all scheduling rules for that employee — daily/weekly hour limits, rest periods, consecutive day limits, weekend rules, and more.

  1. Open the employee's profile and click Edit.
  2. In the Contract dropdown, select the appropriate contract template.
  3. Click Save.

If no contract is assigned, the auto-scheduler cannot enforce scheduling constraints for that employee. See Settings → Contracts for how to create contract templates.

Skills Assignment

Skills determine what activities or queues an employee is qualified to handle. To assign skills:

  1. Open the employee's profile and click Edit.
  2. In the Skills section, check the skills that apply to this employee.
  3. Click Save.

Skills are used by the auto-scheduler to match employees to planning units and activities that require specific qualifications. Manage the skills inventory in Settings → Skills.

PTO & Accommodations

From the People submenu or an employee's profile, you can:

  • View PTO balances — see remaining time-off allowances by category
  • Add PTO entries — manually record time off (approved, denied, or pending)
  • Manage accommodations — record scheduling accommodations such as restricted hours, specific days off, or modified shift lengths. These are respected by the auto-scheduler.

Scheduling

▸

Viewing Schedules

The Scheduling page displays employee schedules in a calendar grid. You can switch between two views:

  • Daily View — shows all employees and their shifts for a single date. Useful for seeing coverage at a glance.
  • Weekly View — shows seven days across columns with employees as rows. Useful for spotting gaps and patterns.

Use the filter controls to narrow by LOB/planning unit, team lead, or employee name. The schedule grid color-codes shifts by day model or activity type.

Creating Individual Shifts

  1. Click on an empty cell in the schedule grid (intersection of an employee row and a date column).
  2. In the shift creation dialog, select a Day Model (shift template) which defines the start time, end time, and break placement.
  3. Optionally adjust the start/end time if the day model allows flexibility.
  4. Optionally assign specific activities to intervals within the shift.
  5. Click Save. The shift appears in the grid as a draft (unpublished).

Auto-Generate Schedules

The auto-scheduler builds optimal schedules based on your configuration:

  1. Click Generate at the top of the scheduling page.
  2. Select the date range to generate (typically one week).
  3. Select the planning units / LOBs to include.
  4. The auto-scheduler uses: demand forecasts, employee availability, assigned contracts (hour limits, rest rules, consecutive day limits), skills, accommodations, PTO, and day model templates.
  5. Review the generated schedule. Shifts appear in draft mode.
  6. Make manual adjustments as needed (drag to move, click to edit).
  7. When satisfied, click Publish.

The auto-scheduler respects all contract rules — minimum rest between shifts, max consecutive days, weekly hour limits, weekend rules, and more. If a constraint cannot be satisfied, the scheduler flags the conflict.

Publishing Schedules

Generated and manually created schedules start in draft mode. Agents cannot see draft schedules.

  1. Review the draft schedule for completeness and coverage.
  2. Click Publish (or select specific dates to publish).
  3. Published schedules become visible to agents in their My Schedule portal.
  4. Agents with notifications enabled receive an alert that their schedule is available.

You can unpublish and make further edits, then re-publish. Agents will see the updated version.

Day Models

Day models are shift templates that define the structure of a work day. Each day model specifies:

  • Shift start and end time (e.g., 08:00 – 16:30)
  • Total paid hours
  • Break placement — which breaks/lunch are included, their offset from shift start, duration, and flexibility window
  • Activities — the default activities during the shift

Set up day models in Settings → Day Models before building schedules. You can create multiple day models for different shift types (morning, afternoon, evening, split shift, short day, etc.).

Shift Swaps & Shift Bidding

▸

{{ brand_name }} supports three self-service workflows that give agents flexibility while keeping admins and supervisors in control: Shift Bidding, Shift Swaps, and VTO/OT Posts.

Shift Bidding

Shift bidding lets admins/supervisors post open shifts that agents can bid on. The admin then selects the winning bid.

Admin / Supervisor Workflow

  1. Navigate to Scheduling or the Shift Bidding section.
  2. Click Post Open Shift.
  3. Select the date, day model (shift template), and planning unit/LOB.
  4. Optionally set required skills, a bidding deadline, and a description/note.
  5. Click Post. The open shift becomes visible to all eligible agents in their portal.
  6. As agents submit bids, you can see the list of bidders with their names and timestamps.
  7. Review the bids. Consider each agent's existing schedule (hours worked, rest requirements, skills) to avoid conflicts.
  8. Click Select Winner next to the chosen agent. The shift is assigned to that agent.
  9. The winning agent receives a notification. Other bidders are notified the shift has been filled.

Agent Workflow

  1. Open your Agent Portal and look for the Open Shifts or Shift Bidding section.
  2. Browse available open shifts. Each listing shows the date, time, LOB, and any required skills.
  3. Click Bid on any shift you want.
  4. Wait for the admin/supervisor to select a winner. You'll receive a notification with the result.
  5. If your bid is selected, the shift appears on your schedule in My Schedule.

Shift Swaps

Shift swaps let agents trade shifts with each other, subject to supervisor/admin approval.

Requesting a Swap (Agent)

  1. Go to My Schedule in the agent portal.
  2. Click on the shift you want to swap and select Request Swap.
  3. Choose one of two options:
    • Direct swap — select a specific colleague and one of their shifts to trade with.
    • Open swap — post your shift as available for any eligible agent to pick up.
  4. Add an optional note (e.g., reason for the swap).
  5. Click Submit.

Accepting a Swap (Other Agent)

  1. If you receive a direct swap request, you'll get a notification. Open it to see the details.
  2. Review the proposed trade — your shift and the other agent's shift.
  3. Click Accept or Decline.
  4. For open swaps, browse the Available Swaps section in your portal and click Accept on any swap you want.

Approving a Swap (Admin / Supervisor)

  1. Once both agents have agreed, the swap request appears in the Approvals queue.
  2. Review the swap: check both agents' schedules for conflicts, overtime, rest period violations, and skill coverage.
  3. Click Approve to finalize. Both agents' schedules are updated automatically.
  4. Click Deny with an optional reason if the swap would cause a coverage or compliance issue.
  5. Both agents receive a notification with the decision.

VTO / OT Posts

Admins can post voluntary time off (VTO) or overtime (OT) opportunities for agents to sign up.

Posting VTO or OT (Admin / Supervisor)

  1. Navigate to Real-Time Management or Scheduling.
  2. Click Post VTO or Post OT.
  3. Select the date, time window, planning unit, and number of slots available.
  4. Click Post. The opportunity appears in agents' portals.
  5. As agents sign up, review the list and confirm selections. Slots are filled on a first-come basis or by admin selection, depending on your configuration.

Signing Up for VTO or OT (Agent)

  1. Check the VTO/OT Opportunities section in your agent portal.
  2. Browse available postings. Each shows the date, time, and number of open slots.
  3. Click Sign Up to express interest.
  4. You'll receive a notification confirming whether your signup was accepted.
  5. If accepted, your schedule is updated: VTO removes your shift (or portion of it), OT adds an extension or extra shift.

Forecasting

▸

Overview

The Forecasting module helps predict future workload demand. Upload historical volume data to generate forecasts that feed into capacity planning and schedule generation.

Viewing Forecasts

Select a planning unit (LOB) and date range to view forecast data. Charts show predicted vs. actual volumes by interval. Toggle between daily totals and interval-level detail (15-minute or 30-minute granularity).

Importing Data

Use the Data Import section to upload historical contact volumes. The system uses this data to calculate trends and generate forecasts. You can import via CSV upload or configure an API connection for automated data feeds (see Settings → API Connections).

Forecast Methods

Choose from five forecasting methods:

  • Auto (Best Fit) — automatically selects the best method based on your data by testing each and choosing the lowest error
  • Weighted Trend — applies recency-weighted trends to historical patterns, giving more influence to recent weeks
  • Moving Average — smooths recent history into a forward projection. Good for stable workloads with minimal seasonality
  • Holt-Winters — captures seasonal patterns for cyclical workloads (e.g., monthly billing cycles, holiday spikes)
  • ML (Gradient Boosting) — machine learning model trained on your data with day-of-week, seasonality, and lag features. Best when you have 3+ months of history

Mid-Day Reforecast

When today's actual volumes differ from the original forecast, the Mid-Day Reforecast tool adjusts the remaining intervals in real time. Open the reforecast panel at the bottom of the Forecasting page.

How it works: The system compares actual data received so far today against the original forecast, computes a trend factor, and applies it to remaining intervals to give you a revised projection.

Three strategies:

  • Blended (Recommended) — 70% ratio scaling + 30% time-weighted adjustment using the most recent intervals. Best for most situations.
  • Ratio Scaling — multiplies remaining intervals by the overall actual-to-forecast ratio. Good when the trend is consistent throughout the day.
  • Delta Shift — adds the average absolute difference to remaining intervals. Useful when volumes shift by a fixed amount rather than a percentage.

Variance Alerts

Click Check Alerts to scan all LOBs for significant deviations between forecast and actuals. Alerts are classified as:

  • Critical — deviation ≥ 25%
  • Warning — deviation ≥ 15%
  • Info — deviation ≥ 10%

Adjust the threshold percentage to suit your operation's tolerance.

Export

Use the Export CSV button in the interval detail table to download the reforecast data for offline analysis or sharing.

Capacity Planning

▸

Capacity Panel

The Capacity Panel shows current staffing levels against requirements for each planning unit. For every interval it displays:

  • Required staff — calculated from forecast volume, service level targets, AHT, and shrinkage
  • Scheduled staff — how many employees are actually scheduled
  • Variance — the difference (positive = overstaffed, negative = understaffed)

Color coding highlights intervals that are significantly over or under the requirement. Use this to identify coverage gaps before publishing schedules.

Capacity Summary

The summary view aggregates capacity data across all planning units, showing overall staffing health for the selected date range. It rolls up interval-level data into daily and weekly totals, making it easy to see trends at a higher level.

Long-Range Planning

Use long-range planning to project headcount needs weeks or months ahead:

  1. Select the planning unit and the future date range.
  2. The system combines your forecast data with planning unit parameters (service level targets, AHT, shrinkage) to calculate required FTEs.
  3. Compare required FTEs against your current headcount to identify hiring needs.
  4. Export the long-range plan for budgeting and workforce planning discussions.

Google Sheets Integration

Capacity data can be pulled from or pushed to connected Google Sheets for collaboration with finance and operations teams:

  1. Go to Settings → Google Sheets.
  2. Enter the Google Sheet key (the long string in the sheet URL between /d/ and /edit).
  3. Share the Google Sheet with the service account email shown on the settings page (give it Editor access).
  4. Test the connection. Once verified, capacity data flows between {{ brand_name }} and the sheet.

Real-Time Management

▸

Real-Time Dashboard

The real-time dashboard provides a live view of your workforce. It displays:

  • Agent Status Board — each agent's current state (available, on call, on break, after-call work, logged out, etc.) with duration in current state
  • Adherence Indicators — green for in-adherence, red for out-of-adherence, showing what the agent is doing versus what they're scheduled for
  • Queue Metrics — current calls in queue, longest wait time, service level percentage (if connected to your ACD/telephony system)
  • Staffing vs. Requirement — real-time comparison of agents available versus the number needed based on current volume

Adherence Monitoring

Adherence compares an agent's actual activity to their scheduled activity:

  • In Adherence — the agent is doing what they're scheduled to do
  • Out of Adherence — the agent's actual state does not match their schedule (e.g., on break when scheduled for calls)

Click on any agent to see a timeline of their adherence for the day, including each state change and its duration.

Live Agent Status

Filter the agent list by planning unit, team lead, or status. Sort by adherence percentage or time in current state to quickly identify agents who may need attention.

VTO / OT Management

When real-time volume deviates from the forecast:

  • Volume lower than expected — post VTO (voluntary time off) opportunities. Agents sign up from their portal, and you approve to release staff early.
  • Volume higher than expected — post OT (overtime) opportunities. Available agents can sign up for extended shifts or come in on their day off.

See the Shift Swaps & Shift Bidding section above for the full VTO/OT posting workflow.

Activities Setup

Activities define everything your workforce does during a shift — productive work, breaks, meetings, training, etc. They are the foundation of scheduling, adherence monitoring, and reporting.

What Activities Are

An activity represents a type of work or non-work time: taking calls, handling emails, lunch break, coaching, team meeting, after-call work, and so on. Each scheduled interval is assigned an activity, and real-time adherence compares what the agent is actually doing against the activity they are scheduled for.

Activity Categories

  • Status Activities — represent agent states like on-call, break, lunch, training, meeting, after-call work, logged out. Used for scheduling and adherence.
  • LOB (Line of Business) Activities — represent the type of work being done, such as inbound calls, outbound calls, email, chat, back-office. Used for routing and workload assignment.

Activity Fields

  • Code — a unique short code for the activity (e.g., "CALL", "BRK", "LUN", "TRN")
  • Label — the display name shown in schedules and reports (e.g., "Inbound Calls", "Break", "Lunch")
  • Color — the color used to represent this activity in schedule grids and charts (hex code or color picker)
  • Productive Flag — whether this activity counts as productive time (yes for calls, email, chat; no for breaks, lunch, meetings)
  • Paid Flag — whether this activity counts as paid time (yes for most activities; no for unpaid lunch, for example)
  • Default Flag — whether this is the default activity assigned to new shift intervals when no specific activity is specified

Offset, Duration & Flexibility Windows

For activities like breaks and lunch that need to be placed at specific points in a shift:

  • Offset — how many minutes after the shift start this activity should begin (e.g., break at 120 minutes = 2 hours into the shift)
  • Duration — how long the activity lasts in minutes (e.g., 15 minutes for a break, 30 minutes for lunch)
  • Flexibility Window — how many minutes earlier or later the activity can be placed by the auto-scheduler. A flexibility of 30 means the break can start anywhere from 30 minutes before to 30 minutes after the offset, giving the scheduler room to optimize coverage.

External IDs

If you integrate with external systems (ACD, telephony, WFM APIs), each activity can have an External ID that maps to the corresponding activity code in the external system. This ensures data flows correctly between {{ brand_name }} and your other tools.

Multi-Activity Support

{{ brand_name }} supports parent/child activity relationships for complex scheduling:

  • A parent activity groups related child activities (e.g., "Customer Service" as a parent with "Inbound Calls", "Email", and "Chat" as children)
  • When the parent is assigned in a schedule, the agent can be routed to any of the child activities based on real-time demand
  • Set up multi-activity by creating the parent activity first, then assigning child activities to it

Creating an Activity Step by Step

  1. Go to Settings → Activities.
  2. Click + Add Activity.
  3. Enter the code, label, and select a color.
  4. Set the category (Status or LOB).
  5. Toggle the productive, paid, and default flags as appropriate.
  6. If this is a break or lunch activity, set the offset, duration, and flexibility window.
  7. If integrating with external systems, enter the external ID.
  8. If this is a child of a multi-activity group, select the parent activity.
  9. Click Save.

Contracts Setup

Contracts are the scheduling rulebook for your employees. Each contract defines the hours, rest periods, and scheduling constraints that {{ brand_name }}'s auto-scheduler enforces. Every employee should be assigned a contract.

Why Contracts Matter

Without a contract, the auto-scheduler has no rules to follow for an employee — it won't know their target hours, maximum consecutive days, required rest between shifts, or weekend rules. Contracts ensure schedules are compliant with labor rules and organizational policy.

General Tab

  • Name — descriptive contract name (e.g., "Full-Time Standard", "Part-Time Weekend")
  • Abbreviation — short code shown in compact views (e.g., "FT-STD", "PT-WE")
  • Color — color used when displaying contract-related information in schedules
  • Contract Type — select full_time or part_time. This classification affects hour targets and may be used in reporting.
  • Days Per Week — how many days per week this contract covers (e.g., 5 for a standard full-time, 3 for a part-time)
  • Workdays Calculation:
    • Flexible — the auto-scheduler chooses which days of the week to schedule, as long as it meets the days-per-week count
    • Fixed — specific days of the week are designated as workdays (e.g., Monday through Friday)

Work Time Tab

Defines hour targets and limits at daily, weekly, and monthly levels:

  • Daily Hours Minimum — shortest allowed shift length (e.g., 4:00 for a minimum 4-hour shift)
  • Daily Hours Target — ideal shift length (e.g., 8:00 for a standard 8-hour day)
  • Daily Hours Maximum — longest allowed shift length (e.g., 10:00)
  • Weekly Hours Minimum — minimum total hours per week (e.g., 32:00)
  • Weekly Hours Target — ideal total hours per week (e.g., 40:00)
  • Monthly Hours Maximum — cap on total hours per month
  • Per-Day Hour Overrides — override the daily target for specific days of the week. Enter values in HH:MM format for Monday through Sunday. Leave blank to use the default daily target. Useful when certain days consistently have longer or shorter shifts.

Scheduling Tab

Controls the auto-scheduler's behavior and scheduling compliance rules. This tab has two groups:

AutoScheduler Parameters:

  • Use Target Work Times — when enabled, the auto-scheduler tries to hit the daily/weekly hour targets exactly (not just stay within min/max)
  • Schedule After Day Off — controls whether the scheduler can place a shift immediately after a day off with no gap
  • Min Days Off Per Week — minimum number of days off the employee must have each week (e.g., 1 or 2)
  • Max Days Off Per Week — maximum days off allowed per week
  • Max Consecutive Work Days — the longest streak of consecutive work days allowed before a day off is required (e.g., 6)
  • Min Consecutive Work Days — the shortest streak of work days (prevents isolated single work days surrounded by days off)
  • Min Rest Hours Between Shifts — minimum gap in hours between the end of one shift and the start of the next (e.g., 11 hours). Prevents back-to-back clopening shifts.

Scheduling Parameters:

  • Max Saturdays Per Month — limit on how many Saturdays an employee can be scheduled in a month
  • Max Sundays Per Month — limit on Sunday scheduling
  • Night Shift Limits — maximum number of consecutive night shifts allowed before a longer rest break
  • Rest Period Rules — additional rest requirements (e.g., extended rest after a night shift rotation)
  • Overtime Thresholds — at what point hours count as overtime (typically after 8 hours daily or 40 hours weekly, but configurable)
  • Holiday Scheduling Rules — whether employees under this contract can be scheduled on designated holidays, and any premium/overtime implications
  • Weekend Rules — policies for weekend scheduling, such as requiring at least one full weekend off per month or alternating weekend schedules

Assigning Contracts to Employees

  1. Create the contract template in Settings → Contracts (see steps above).
  2. Go to People and open the employee's profile.
  3. Click Edit and select the contract from the Contract dropdown.
  4. Click Save. The auto-scheduler will now use this contract's rules when generating schedules for this employee.

Creating a Contract Step by Step

  1. Go to Settings → Contracts.
  2. Click + Add Contract.
  3. Fill in the General tab: name, abbreviation, color, contract type, days per week, workdays calculation mode.
  4. Switch to the Work Time tab: set daily min/target/max hours, weekly min/target, monthly max. Add per-day overrides if needed.
  5. Switch to the Scheduling tab: configure auto-scheduler parameters (target work times, days off, consecutive days, rest hours) and scheduling parameters (weekends, nights, overtime, holidays).
  6. Click Save.

Planning Units

Planning units represent your workload groups or lines of business (LOBs). Each planning unit is a distinct queue or work type that can be forecasted, staffed, and scheduled independently.

Setting Up a Planning Unit

  1. Go to Settings → Planning Units.
  2. Click + Add Planning Unit.
  3. Enter the name (e.g., "Inbound Sales", "Technical Support", "Billing").
  4. Set the business hours for each day of the week — the hours during which this LOB operates. For example, Mon-Fri 08:00-20:00, Sat 09:00-17:00, Sun closed.
  5. Click Save.

Assigning Activities to Planning Units

Each planning unit should have activities associated with it — these define what work happens in this LOB. From the planning unit's settings, add the relevant activities (e.g., inbound calls, email, chat). This lets the scheduler know which activities to assign agents to when they are scheduled into this planning unit.

Planning Unit Parameters

  • Minimum Staffing — the minimum number of agents that must be scheduled at all times during business hours, regardless of forecast demand
  • Service Level Target — the percentage of contacts that should be answered within the target time (e.g., 80% of calls answered within 20 seconds)
  • Service Level Time — the target answer time in seconds
  • Average Handle Time (AHT) — the expected average duration of each contact in seconds
  • Shrinkage — the percentage of scheduled time that agents are unavailable due to breaks, meetings, training, absenteeism, etc. (e.g., 30%). This factor increases the required headcount to compensate.

Support & Troubleshooting

Common Issues & Solutions

Can't log in

  • Verify you're using the correct email address (check for typos).
  • If you've forgotten your password, contact your admin to reset it.
  • If using social login (Google/Microsoft), make sure it's enabled for your organization in Settings.
  • Clear your browser cache and cookies, then try again.
  • If your account has been deactivated, contact your admin to reactivate it.

Schedule not showing for agents

  • Schedules must be published before agents can see them. Check with your supervisor or admin.
  • Make sure the agent's user account is linked to their employee record (Settings → Users).
  • Verify the schedule covers the dates the agent is looking at.

Missing data or empty pages

  • Check that data has been imported for the date range you're viewing.
  • Verify your filters aren't excluding the data — try resetting all filters.
  • For forecasting data, ensure historical volumes have been uploaded via Data Import.

Permission denied errors

  • Your user role may not have access to that feature. Check with your admin.
  • Agents can only access the agent portal sections. Management pages require Supervisor or Admin roles.

Auto-scheduler not generating schedules

  • Ensure employees have contracts assigned (People → employee profile).
  • Verify day models are configured (Settings → Day Models).
  • Check that planning units have business hours set and activities assigned.
  • Review the error messages — the scheduler reports specific constraint violations.

Google Sheets connection not working

  • Verify the sheet key is correct (the string between /d/ and /edit in the sheet URL).
  • Make sure the Google Sheet is shared with the service account email shown in Settings → Google Sheets, with Editor access.
  • Click Test Connection to verify.

Notifications not arriving

  • Check the bell icon — notifications may be there but the badge count didn't update. Try refreshing the page.
  • If using the PWA, ensure browser notifications are allowed for {{ brand_name }}.

Slow page loading

  • Try reducing the page size in tables (use 25 or 50 instead of All).
  • Narrow your date range or filters to reduce the amount of data loaded.
  • Clear your browser cache.
  • Check your internet connection.

Getting More Help
